Reading the map

Every short form on this map, in plain words. While this is open, point at or tap any mark on the map to have it named.

Expected shortfall (ES) 97.5%
The average loss across the worst 2.5% of the 10,000 simulated 30-trading-day outcomes.
VaR 95
The loss that only the worst 5% of those outcomes go beyond.
T+30
Thirty trading days after the last close.
Mean-reverting (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck, OU) Monte Carlo
The simulation engine. Each path moves the book's beta and volatility day by day, with a pull back toward their long-run levels - an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (volatility moves on a log scale).
AR(1)
The one-step rule the model is fitted with: tomorrow's value is a constant, plus a fraction of today's, plus a random shock.
Long-run vol
The volatility level the simulation pulls the book back toward, estimated from its own history.
Calm · Baseline · Stressed
Baseline uses the shock sizes fitted to the book. Stressed makes them 1.4 times larger and adds 0.10 to their correlation; Calm makes them 0.7 times as large. A stated policy, not an estimate.
Beta
How much the book moves with the market, measured over the last 63 trading days.
Vol
How big the book's daily swings are, over the last 21 trading days, stated per year.
Daily closes (EOD)
End-of-day adjusted closing prices from Yahoo Finance - not real-time.
Days fitted
The trading days on which both the book's beta and its volatility could be measured; the model is fitted on them.
Perimeter and breach
The perimeter is a risk-limit line at 3 times today's volatility (kept between 35% and 90%) or at beta plus 0.8 (capped at 1.9; mirrored for a short book). A breach is a path that touches it at any point in the 30 days.
Rings 68 · 95 · 99.7%
The share of simulated end points that falls inside each ring.
Shock correlation · leverage effect
How the beta and volatility shocks move together, and how a day's return moves with the volatility shock (usually negative: falling prices tend to lift volatility).
Shocks widened (k)
A factor between 1 and 3, measured by replaying the book's own history with only earlier data, that widens the shocks until the 68% ring really holds about 68% of 30-day outcomes.
Drawdown cushion
How many percentage points less the pair fell at its worst than the flyer alone, on replayed real days: "shallower" means it fell less, "deeper" more. In the demo, the crisis cushion compares simulated worst-case losses instead.
Flyer · anchor
The flyer is a high-volatility holding; the anchor is a steadier, defensive name paired with it, weighted so each carries an equal share of the risk.
ES 97.5 · daily
In the pairing panel: the same worst-2.5% average, measured on real daily returns instead of simulated 30-day paths.
